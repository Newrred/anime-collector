1. Check before publishing

People who are not signed in can view content you publish. Quick sharing of a memory publishes the title and image shown on the screen, while your thoughts and card tags remain private. For a board, you publish after checking the selected memories and fields in the preview. A mini-home publishes the selected display name, introduction, and public boards or featured memories.

Preparing an image does not by itself publish it. Preparing a public copy does not remove the private record. Before pressing the publish button, check the displayed items and the publication scope. Confirming the right to publish and choosing the scope of personal data disclosure are separate checks, each of which is needed. Signup or acceptance of the Terms does not replace them.

Other people may save, copy or redistribute public content. Withdrawing publication cannot retrieve copies those people have already received.

2. Content that may be published

MOEMOA’s public spaces are intended for a general audience. You may not publish adult or illegal content, nonconsensual intimate imagery, child sexual exploitation material, images that infringe rights, harassment, threats, deception, or content that discloses another person’s personal data without authorization. Nonconsensual intimate depictions digitally created using a real person are also prohibited.

When publishing an image you created, confirm that you created it and have the necessary publication rights. If it contains someone else’s work or a real person, additional permissions may be needed. Third-party images, including animation screenshots and fan art created by other people, must go through verification of public-use permission and the service’s approval process. Merely saving an image, crediting its source or selecting a checkbox does not obtain permission. Official cover images permitted by the service may be used only when you select them directly in the designated selection screen.

The operator’s review or successful image preparation by the service does not guarantee possession of every legal right. Rights in the content remain with its creator or original rights holder. The limited permission needed to provide the service is governed by the Terms of Use {{TERMS_URL}}.

3. General reports and urgent reports

Please report problems through the reporting and blocking features on public screens or to godburgundy@gmail.com. You can report without an account. A public page URL or other information that helps locate the content, a description of the problem and a way to contact you will help us review it. Do not send passwords, sign-in codes or tokens. Do not attach unnecessary identity documents or a new copy of the intimate image at issue.

If you or an authorized representative wish to request removal of nonconsensual intimate imagery, use the Urgent Reporting Guide {{NCII_URL}}. This procedure is separate from general inquiries and copyright reports. Prior contact with the person who published the material or a police report is not a prerequisite for submitting the request.

The operator may hide content, withdraw publication, restrict features or close an account after considering the nature, urgency and repetition of the violation and any necessary legal measures. We may act first to prevent urgent harm. To the extent permitted by safety considerations and law, we explain the reason and how to appeal. Blocking another user does not by itself remove that user’s posts for everyone.

4. Requests to suspend reproduction or transmission under Korean copyright law

A rights holder claiming infringement of copyright or another right protected by the Korean Copyright Act, or a properly authorized representative, may make a request under Article 103 of that Act. The designated recipient for these statutory requests is {{COPYRIGHT_RECIPIENT_NAME_DEPT}}. Contact details: godburgundy@gmail.com and {{COPYRIGHT_PHONE_FAX}}. Postal address: {{COPYRIGHT_POSTAL_ADDRESS}}.

Please include the work you believe has been infringed; a URL or other location information sufficient to find the material on the service; your status as a rights holder and the grounds for the alleged infringement; necessary contact details and signature; and the supporting material required by law. We explain how to submit the current statutory suspension request form and necessary identity or representative evidence. Do not send a complete original identity document through a general inquiry. Where the law permits an alternative to, or exemption from, particular supporting evidence, we follow that procedure. We do not disregard an infringement notice that sufficiently identifies the material merely because it does not use a particular URL format.

For a request meeting the applicable requirements, we immediately suspend reproduction and transmission and notify the claimant and the relevant uploader within the statutory period after suspension. A request form or necessary allegations may be passed to the other party under the statutory procedure, so do not include personal data unrelated to the case.

An uploader may request resumption within 30 days of receiving the suspension notice by substantiating that the use is based on lawful rights. Within 3 days of receiving the resumption request, the operator assesses the claimed lawful rights and, where they are substantiated, sets a scheduled resumption date and notifies the claimant. The scheduled date is set in the period from 7 days after receipt of the resumption request through day 14. Where a statutory restriction on resumption applies, such as notification of legal proceedings under the law, we follow that procedure. False or improper statutory suspension or resumption requests may give rise to liability under applicable law.

5. Repeat infringements and appeals against operator measures

The operator may restrict or terminate public features or accounts in response to repeated rights infringements, as appropriate to the circumstances. We consider repetition, intent, harm, previous measures and appeal outcomes. The law does not prescribe the same number of warnings for every case, and serious cases may result in immediate restrictions. Withdrawn or incorrectly determined reports are not continually counted as confirmed infringements.

If you believe a measure was incorrect, send the request reference, relevant post, measure taken, reasons for review and necessary supporting information to godburgundy@gmail.com or through the Rights and Appeals Guide {{RIGHTS_URL}}. A general moderation appeal may differ from a statutory Korean copyright resumption request or a US DMCA counter-notice, so we explain the routes separately. Even if copyright permission is confirmed, restrictions based on nonconsensual intimate imagery, another personal data infringement, or a separate legal or safety reason may remain in place.

6. Scope of withdrawing public access

Saving a memory as private removes its public copies from public boards and mini-homes. Withdrawing a board stops that board’s publication; making a mini-home private stops the mini-home’s publication. Making only the mini-home private may leave separately published boards public. Personal records and private copies remain. If you want all of the material deleted, use the separate deletion or account closure feature or make a rights request.

Optional module: US DMCA copyright notices and counter-notices

Notices and counter-notices under US copyright law may be sent to this designated agent: {{DMCA_AGENT_DISPLAY}}. Registration details: {{DMCA_DIRECTORY_URL}}.

A copyright notice must include the following: a physical or electronic signature of the rights holder or authorized representative; identification of the protected work; identification and location information for the disputed material; sufficient contact information; a statement of good-faith belief that the use is not authorized by the rights holder, its agent or the law; and a statement under penalty of perjury that the information is accurate and the person making the notice is authorized to act for the rights holder. We act expeditiously on notices meeting the requirements and give the uploader the necessary information.

An uploader who believes material was removed through mistake or misidentification may submit a DMCA counter-notice. It must include a physical or electronic signature; identification of the removed material and its location before removal; a statement under penalty of perjury of a good-faith belief that removal resulted from mistake or misidentification; name, address and telephone number; and a statement consenting to the jurisdiction of the relevant US federal district court and accepting service of process from the claimant or its agent. If the uploader’s address is outside the United States, the statutory requirement concerning a US judicial district in which the service provider may be found applies. The counter-notice may be passed to the claimant.

After receiving a valid counter-notice, we follow the statutory resumption procedure within the 10–14 business day period unless an exception applies, such as notification of an action seeking an order restraining the alleged infringement. Restrictions based on separate safety, personal data or illegal-content grounds are reviewed independently. Describing this procedure does not guarantee that the service meets every condition for a limitation of liability.
